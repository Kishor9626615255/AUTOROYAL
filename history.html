The History
of Automobiles

From a horseless carriage in 1885 to a 1,000-horsepower electric throne — the greatest mechanical journey ever told.

— Through the Ages —
A Century of Legends
1885

The Birth of the Automobile

Karl Benz patented the Benz Patent-Motorwagen in 1885 — widely regarded as the world's first true automobile powered by a gasoline engine. Running on three wheels with a 0.75 hp engine, it reached a top speed of 16 km/h. The age of the automobile had begun.

⚙ First gasoline-powered automobile — 0.75 hp, 16 km/h
1908

The Model T & Mass Production

Henry Ford introduced the Model T in 1908 and revolutionised the world with the moving assembly line in 1913. What once took 12 hours to build now took 93 minutes. Cars transitioned from luxury items to everyday transport, forever changing civilisation.

🏭 15 million Model T's built — $850 original price
1960s

The Golden Age of Muscle & Speed

The 1960s roared with the most iconic cars ever made. Ford Mustang, Chevrolet Camaro, Dodge Charger — American muscle defined an era. Across the Atlantic, Ferrari and Lamborghini waged war at Le Mans and on the road. Horsepower became the currency of cool.

🏁 Ford GT40 won Le Mans 4 consecutive years (1966–1969)
1997

The Hybrid Revolution Begins

Toyota launched the Prius in Japan in 1997 — the world's first mass-produced hybrid vehicle. Combining a petrol engine with an electric motor, it sparked the green revolution in motoring. By 2023, over 20 million Prius units had been sold worldwide.

🌿 20M+ Prius units sold globally by 2023
2008

Tesla & the Electric Age

Tesla's Roadster in 2008 proved electric cars could be fast, desirable, and practical. The Model S in 2012 shattered every preconception — luxury, range, performance. By 2024, Tesla became the world's most valuable car company, valued above Toyota, BMW and Mercedes combined.

⚡ Tesla Model S — first 400+ km range production EV
Today

The Autonomous & Electric Future

Today's automobiles are rolling supercomputers — capable of driving themselves, updating over the air, and communicating with infrastructure. By 2030, analysts predict 50% of new car sales will be electric. The question is no longer whether cars will transform — but how fast.

🤖 40M+ EVs on global roads as of 2024
